### A leg near straight keeps its foot the way the clip has it

- **The two-bone solve fades its turn onto the pole's plane as the limb
  straightens** (`ae3d.ik`, `solve_two_bone`, #781). It turned the limb's
  present bend plane onto the pole's, whatever its bend. A limb near
  straight has no plane, only a millimetre's noise: a knee 1 mm before the
  hip-ankle line and 1 mm behind it gave opposite planes, so the limb was
  turned 0 degrees one frame and 180 the next about its own line, and the
  foot on its tip with it. A walk's toe swung 23 cm in a frame at a heel
  strike, one of the bone snaps that failed Siltreach's play gate. The
  turn now goes by the square of the bend's sine over STRAIGHT_BEND (10
  degrees), so a millimetre's bend is turned by under a degree; the bend
  about the pole's axis puts the knee on the pole's side either way.
  - tests/test_ik: a leg all but straight, its knee 1 mm before the line
    and then 1 mm behind it, keeps its toe 14 cm ahead of the ankle both
    times (16 cm behind it the second time before).
