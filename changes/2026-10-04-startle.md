### Motion: the arms answer a hard blow at once

- A blow past what steps can catch (it gives the whole body 2.6 m/s or
  more: 335 N s on the 129 kg ragdoll) is answered by the arms at once
  (#606). For 0.35 s they're thrown forward and a little down, the way the
  blow sends the body, at three times their muscle. Then the protective
  fall takes them, or, if it doesn't come, the clip does.
  - Watched in the showcase from the side, a figure shoved hard from behind
    used to have its arms flung back over its head for the first quarter
    second. Now they're out ahead from the first frames, and the hands
    take the landing.
  - Forwards, the protected head meets the ground at 0.12 m/s on average
    (0.62 before), the slower of the pair in 30 of 30. Sideways it's 1.16
    m/s (0.90 before), still 30 of 30.
  - `test_falls` holds the forward mean under 0.5 m/s.
- Only a protective figure startles (`set_protective`). Bracing waits
  while a startle has the arms: from 340 N s, thrown forward and down they
  met the wall sooner, and the head slower, than aimed at it at shoulder
  height. In `test_brace` the braced head is clear or slower in 9 of 9, at
  0.54 m/s on average.
- `tools/motion_showcase.ae` builds without a warning (an unused variable
  in the camera's update).
- The showcase reports each cycle on its own: whether each figure stands
  at the end, the steps it took in the cycle, whether it braced, and
  whether it fell. Its step-back figure is shoved at 150 N s: the box-man
  fixture steps back out of 150 and falls from 180, where `test_balance`'s
  rig holds 180 (#606).
