### Motion: a figure down on its front crawls

`motion.crawl(m, way)` makes a POWERED figure lying on its front drag
itself along the ground toward `way` on its forearms, as a soldier crawls
(#625). `motion.stop_crawl(m)` ends it and gives every bone back its grip;
`motion.crawling(m)` reports it.

- **A stroke a second.** The elbows lift and reach 0.25 m ahead of the
  shoulders, then plant and draw back 0.15 m behind them, the forearms
  along the ground.
- **Weight shifts through grip.** Reaching, the arms slide and the trunk
  and legs hold; pulling, the other way about. A person does this by
  moving their weight between the forearms and the belly.
- **The pull is a muscle's, not a hand of god's.** While a forearm is
  planted, the arm carries 0.45 of the body's weight in force from it to
  the chest, as an equal and opposite pair. It moves the body only as far
  as the forearm's grip on the ground holds. With aims alone, straight
  arms or bent, the arms propped the chest up and slid back under it, and
  the body went backwards (-1.0 m in 10 s).
- **Measured** on the box man, struck down at 2 to 4.5 m/s from behind or
  from the left (22 falls). It ends ahead of where it started in all 22,
  and 2 m or more on in 10 s in 17, with the head within 20 degrees of the
  way. On its side with an arm under it, it barely moves (0.1 m).
  `tests/test_crawl.ae` holds two of them to 2 to 4.5 m.

`AE3D_CRAWL=1` in the motion showcase makes the falling figure crawl once
it lies on its front.
