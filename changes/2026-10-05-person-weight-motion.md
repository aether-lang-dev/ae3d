### Motion: active ragdolls retuned to a person's weight

With figures weighing what a person does (#626), the balance and its
reactions are retuned. The 1200 N·m balance assist had been holding up a
138.5 kg figure. Several catches and protective moves only worked because
of it.

- **Pushes are speeds.** Every motion suite pushes with the speed it gives
  the whole body: impulse = speed × `physics.ragdoll_mass`. The tests then
  mean the same at any weight.
- **The assist the feet can hold.** The pelvis anchor is capped at
  m·g·0.4. At 0.3 a figure that had caught itself hung bent 16° over one
  toe for two seconds (#638). From behind it holds 0.8 m/s standing and
  1.2 m/s stepping.
- **Quicker steps.** Steps take 0.24 s, and a stagger steps again 4 cm off
  its feet. The step aims where the capture point will be when the foot
  lands. In a stagger the feet go in turn. Sideways, the near foot used to
  step again and again, and from 1.2 m/s the figure fell.
- **Stepping back only when needed.** The capture point must leave the feet
  by 0.35 m before a step back (#606). Now the figure stands 0.6 m/s from in
  front and steps back out of 0.8. Before, it fell from 0.6 stepping. A
  figure risen from face down no longer steps off backwards and falls.
- **The protective fall comes in.** It blends in over 0.15 s:
  - the knees' give, the spine's curl and the head's tuck;
  - the arms' reach, from where they hang, with their strength ramping from
    the lean the fall began at.

  Before, it came in all at once at 0.6 rad. That threw a staggering figure
  upright in four frames, its chest spinning at 14 rad/s and its arms
  flailing 21° a frame. A figure that can still step falls only past
  0.6 rad.
- **Bracing.** Braced, the arms take five times their muscle. From 2.25 to
  2.55 m/s toward a wall, the head never meets it. The unbraced twin's
  head hits at 0.9 to 3.6 m/s.
- **Writhing face down.** Less hip and more knee, so the heels lift behind.
  Drawn up at the hips, the knees pushed the figure 0.39 m along the
  ground.
- **Blows.** A blow's weakness takes its share of the capped assist, not of
  1200 N·m. A tap on the hip again leaves the balance nearly whole.
