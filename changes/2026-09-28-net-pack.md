### Multiplayer: UDP, deltas against what each client acknowledged, a budget a client, ragdolls on every peer, Play in the editor, and the same horde on every platform

- `ae3d.net` has a UDP transport (#473), `host_udp` and `join_udp` beside the loopback and TCP, over `ae3d.netudp`, a connection on `std.udp` built as Quake 3's netchan and Glenn Fiedler's articles build one.
  - Every packet acknowledges the newest received and the 32 before it; a burst is acknowledged every 16 packets and a reordered one on its own.
  - A reliable, ordered channel: a window of 1,024, resent on RFC 6298's timeout (or half the round trip again, where that is more) growing by half with every resend, the round trip seeded by the handshake, slices for a message larger than a packet.
  - Unreliable channels a message kind, sequenced (a late snapshot is dropped) or not (inputs), fragments for a message larger than a datagram; datagrams of 1,200 bytes at most.
  - A challenge handshake whose salt every packet carries, a timeout, a disconnect said three times; `on_leave` and `disconnected`.
  - The link simulated per endpoint for tests: latency, jitter and loss, reliable datagrams lost too.
  - `tests/test_net_udp.ae`, 15 checks over loopback UDP with 50 ± 20 ms and 10% loss: every honk and score once and in order, a 100 KB block whole, snapshots never backwards, the whole first one in fragments, no datagram over 1,200 bytes, prediction within the TCP run's centimetre (0.0009 mm), the moving objects within the chord of the longest gap; a leave heard in 0.07 ms, a silent host given up at 1.02 s, a handshake nobody answers at 0.52 s.
- Snapshots are quantised and deltas against the newest one each client acknowledged (#475): positions in tenths of a millimetre as zigzag varints of their change, a rotation's three smallest components in 48 bits, the client's own player at full precision; a ring of 32 a client on each side and a whole snapshot past it; an object that starts to move held where it stood until the tick before; acknowledgements riding on the client's inputs. Protocol 5.
- A client's input carries every command the host has not acknowledged, a repeated one as a bit, where it carried the newest four: the host never has a gap to skip, so no loss or reordering costs a correction (`tests/test_players.ae`, 40% lost: 0.0002 mm, where the four gave 100 mm), and it costs 1.47 KB a second where the four cost 3.06.
  - `tests/test_net_delta.ae`: over UDP with 10% loss every snapshot is the host's state at its tick, 23,762 object states to the bit; a still scene costs 0.5 KB a second where whole snapshots cost 23.9 and the floats 46.0, a walking player 0.67 against 24.4, fifty moving objects 19.5 against 48.4 and 91.4.
  - Sixteen objects on a perfect link cost a client 6.05 KB a second where the floats cost 14.9; the host of `tests/test_net_events.ae` sends three clients 3.5 KB a second where it sent 7.3.
- A client is sent what is relevant to it within its budget, most urgent first (#474): `set_relevant` (always, never, near), `set_priority`, `set_budget` and `set_client_budget`; a priority growing 1 + 10 / d a tick while an object waits; `worst_wait`, `largest_snapshot`, `whole_snapshots`, `traffic` by kind.
  - `tests/test_net_budget.ae`: 500 objects and four clients; every snapshot within its client's budget (386 of 400, 249 of 250 bytes), the object at a player waiting a tick at most, the slowest relevant one four, a far one never sent, the crate ahead held 59.5 m off with a view of 50 m.
- A networked horde's struck zombies go to active ragdolls on every peer (#466): the horde's `hand_over` and `rejoin` are inputs at a tick, the out flags in its hash and a joiner's state; `ae3d.nethandover` has the host's pool simulate the ragdoll and every client pose a puppet of its own from its bones' snapshots (`handover.puppet`). `horde.separate_exact_out` passes the handed-over by.
  - `tests/test_net_handover.ae`: out at the same tick on every peer, every hash the host's through the rejoin, a late joiner with the flags from its first tick, the pelvis within 2 cm on 359 of 360 frames, never both the zombie and its figure drawn, a killed one compacted on every peer at one tick, a moved one keeping its figure; 4.4 KB a ragdoll a second.
- The editor plays a scene as a host and up to four clients (#476): each an engine of its own, the host's simulated as Simulate does, a client's with the scene's static bodies and the simulation's floors; the link's latency, jitter and loss from sliders; a bar over the viewport to show each world, whose player the keys walk, predicted and reconciled; Stop frees every session and world. Simulate's stop now takes a ragdoll's twelve bone objects out of the engine and the renderer. The bounded run (`AE3D_EDITOR_PLAY=2`) reports the worlds drawn, the players against the host and what Stop gave back, and `ci.sh` asserts it.
- The same horde on every platform (#441): every build compiles with `-ffp-contract=off`, and `tests/test_determinism.ae` hashes the networked horde at every tick and aephysics's falling ragdolls at every step; every CI runner writes its trail, and a `determinism` job compares Linux, Windows and macOS arm64 line for line: 513 lines, the same to the bit.
- `physics.ray_distance`, `ray_static_distance` and `capsule_clear`; `net.alive` counts what is not freed.
- `examples/net_walk.ae` and `examples/net_cars.ae` run over UDP; `AE3D_NET_TRANSPORT=tcp` takes the stream.
- docs/networking.md: UDP, what each client is sent, the handover on every peer, the same on every platform, and every measurement.
