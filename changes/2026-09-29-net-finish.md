### Multiplayer finished: fields of a script's state, relevance by sight, the link on the agent channel, authority, smooth corrections, exact prediction, and the street played by sixteen

- `net.replicate` replicates a field of a script's state beside an object's transform (#486): an int, a float in steps of a precision or as its 64 bits, a bool, a Vec3, a text of up to 31 bytes the session keeps (`set_text`, `text`). The host reads each every tick; a field goes in its object's record against what the client acknowledged, only when it changes -- a mask and each change as a zigzag varint -- and whole, with its kinds and precisions, where the client has none, so a client reads an object's fields before the reliable word that makes it. A client writes every field from every snapshot it takes, never from one older than it applied, and `on_changed` calls a hook once a change, in tick order; `fields_known` says what it holds.
  - `tests/test_net_fields.ae`, over loopback UDP at 50 ± 20 ms and 10% lost: 49,807 field values of twelve crates, a mover, three players and a rocket against the host's at each frame's snapshot tick, none different, bit for bit; 564 changes, 564 hooks on each client in order, none missed or extra; a client's own player's health reaches it; within a budget of 120 bytes a field and its transform arrive in one snapshot every time; six still fields on twenty moving objects cost the same bytes to the byte as none.
- Relevance by what each client can see (#487): a client sends its view (`set_view`, `set_camera_view`) with its inputs, 18 bytes when it has moved 25 cm or turned a degree, its eye from its player's place where it has one, which the host keeps there as the player goes; the host judges each object against the frustum and, inside it, a ray from the eye against its static world (`set_occluders`, `physics.ray_static_hits`), a ray an object every sixth tick. What a client sees gathers priority four times as fast, what it cannot a quarter as fast; a seen object that waited a tick goes ahead of the rest, and so does anything that waited a second and a half. A record no budget's room could hold goes alone rather than wait for ever.
  - `tests/test_net_sight.ae`, 500 moving objects in a street of buildings, two clients looking opposite ways, 900 bytes a snapshot: what a client sees waits a tick at most (4 by distance alone), what it cannot 18 ticks at most and none left unsent; turning round, the 125 objects newly in view all sent within a tick; 69% of the bytes on what is seen, against 25% by distance alone; 228 rays a tick for two clients.
- The link measured on every transport, both ways (#488): ten pings a second give the round trip and its jitter (RFC 6298, integer microseconds) and the loss over the last fifty; every byte counted by peer, kind and way, with rates over windows of a second (`rate`, `link_bytes`, `round_trip_us`, `jitter_us`, `loss_ppm`, `budget_of`, `worst_wait_any`, `correction_um`, `commands_asked`). UDP counts each connection's headers and resends apart (`netudp.conn_headers`, `conn_resent`).
  - `net.stats` and `net.set_link` on the agent channel, in the engine's command table and answered by `ae3d.net` through `agent.provide`; every figure a whole number in the unit its name says. `tests/test_agent_net.ae` asks a host and a client over the channel, held still, and compares all 115 figures with the sessions' own, twice: none differs; `net.set_link` from 20 ms to 100 ms moves the reported round trip by 155 to 165 ms for the 160 added.
- Who simulates what (#413): a script's `fixed_update` runs only where its object is simulated -- on the host for what it owns, on the owning client for its player -- the engine passing a remote object's by (`behaviour.object_remote`); a dynamic body on a client's copy is kinematic, driven where the session puts it (`physics.rigidbody_set_kind`); `net.release` gives them back. A remote object is corrected smoothly: after the view ran past its newest sample, it is drawn on from where it stood, its error falling to a third every tenth of a second (`set_smoothing`), never snapped.
  - `tests/test_net_authority.ae`: a crate's script's `fixed_update` 240 times on the host and none on either client, its `update` on all three; each player's only on its own client; the crate's body kinematic on a client, within 0.05 mm of the host's as it is pushed; through 400 ms of lost snapshots an object at 6 m/s moves 0.40 m in a step at most where it snapped 2.06 m, and is 0.65 mm off 0.8 s later.
- Prediction exact to the bit: a player's place and fall are kept after every move as the snapshot carries them, in 32-bit floats, on the host and in the client's prediction and replays alike, so a reconciliation moves nothing wherever the player goes -- rounded only on the wire, a walker crossing the seam between two of the street's blocks, floors 4 mm apart, was corrected 5.3 mm each time. `tests/test_players.ae` and `test_net_udp.ae`, which reconciled by 0.4 and 0.9 µm, and the street, by 3 µm, now reconcile by 0.
- Commands (protocol 7): each goes in as many inputs as the link's measured loss asks for all of them to be lost once in a million commands (by the rule of succession over the pings judged: every unacknowledged one before any is, four once fifty are, never fewer than three, every one on a link losing half); the host, holding a later command, says in its snapshot which it lacks, and the client sends it again, so none is ever lost for good. A command goes as what it is less the one before and that one's change, a jump as a bit. A reliable message's acknowledgement rides on the next packet going, or goes alone 30 ms on. A driver in the street sent 5.7 KB a second at its worst with every unacknowledged command in every input; with all of this, 3.5 at most; a walker 2.4, where it sent 3.2. `tests/test_players.ae` holds the repair: a burst that loses every message carrying some commands, the host asks, every command applied, no correction.
- `examples/net_street.ae`: the zombie street played by a host and fifteen bot clients over loopback UDP, 50 ms each way and 2% lost (#489) -- two of them driving the cars, each car its driver's player, predicted and reconciled, with its speed, horn and driver as fields; the rest walking; a horde of 400 of the pipeline's zombie simulated on every peer and shoved by the cars; `AE3D_NET=host` and `join:addr` across machines, the first two to join at the wheel. The play runs on a clock of its own, in fixed steps of a sixtieth of a second, as many a frame as the wall clock has gone (up to five seconds of them), so how fast the window draws does not come into it: every run gives the same figures to the byte, on a GPU at 140 frames a second and under Mesa's llvmpipe at five or six frames in the run -- where one step a frame had the Linux runner's software renderer end the play before a bot was welcomed. `ci.sh` runs ten seconds of it: every client under 20 KB a second down (6.6 in its worst second) and 4 up (a walker 2.28 to 2.30, a driver 3.31 and 3.36), every client's horde the host's at each of the 4,156 ticks compared a run and the 4,627 seals checked, the cars' speed and horn the host's at every snapshot's tick (9,167 a run) and their driver named on all fifteen, both cars round at 5.2 m/s on average, no reconciliation moving any player; the host's own work 0.05 ms a frame alone and 0.12 to 0.15 with fifteen clients.
- Leaks, found as macOS's `leaks` finds them and checked here by a leak check that wraps malloc and marks what is still reachable at exit: the session's error texts are its own and freed when the next replaces them (a refused field left three behind); its agent answers free the texts they copy; `tests/test_agent_net.ae` frees its players on the path where there is no window; `tests/test_net_udp.ae` the second client's send times; the engine the `AE3D_API` value it reads; the street what it lent the engine (every model it drew, its lamps), the clips it baked its walk from, its pose bank and the environment's values. Every network suite, and the street's bounded run, as a host, a network host and with its bots: none left.
- `tests/test_net_udp.ae` holds the drawing to the chord of the longest interval between two snapshots the client took, by the host's times for them, rather than to the run of them lost alone: on a loaded machine a late frame put a tick a few milliseconds after its time and failed it by 0.1 to 1.2 mm, three runs in sixteen here and main's test one in eight; and allows past it only what the objects went while the view had run past its newest snapshot. Sixteen runs, none failed.
