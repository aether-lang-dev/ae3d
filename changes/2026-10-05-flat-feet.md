### Physics: a fitted ragdoll's feet lie flat under its shins

Every ragdoll stood on tiptoe (#626, #606). aephysics' reference human
pitches each foot capsule about 50° off the shin: from the toe at ankle
height to a heel 8.5 cm up the shin. Fitting it to a rig turned the foot to
the rig's toe but never levelled it. A figure standing on upright shins
stood on its toes:
- its heels were 8.5 to 9 cm off the ground;
- its support was a line under the toes;
- the 1200 N·m balance assist held it up where its feet could not.

This held for `test_balance`'s rig and for the box man alike.

`fit_to_rig` now lays each fitted foot flat (`level_feet`). The foot runs
perpendicular to the shin, at the toe end's height along it, toward where
the rig's toe points: `FOOT_AHEAD` (12 cm) in front of the shin and
`FOOT_BEHIND` (5 cm) behind, both in proportion to the shin (`FOOT_SHIN`,
the reference's 0.45 m). Settled, both ends of each foot are on the ground,
and the centre of mass is about 5 cm ahead of the heel end. A person
carries it a few centimetres ahead of the ankle.

Measured on `test_balance`'s rig at a person's 75 kg, with the balance
assist capped at m·g·0.3 (an experiment, not in this change):

| m/s held | feet in place: behind / front / side | stepping: behind / front / side |
|---|---|---|
| on tiptoe | 0.4 / 0.2 / 0.6 | 0.4 / 0.2 / 0.6 |
| flat, under the body | 0.6 / 0.4 / 0.6 | up to 1.4 / 0.4 / up to 1.2 |

With flat feet, stepping catches what standing cannot under an assist the
feet can bear. On tiptoe it added nothing.

The reference human that `physics.ragdoll` makes before it is dressed keeps
the reference's feet. aephysics is unchanged.
