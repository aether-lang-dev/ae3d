### A shove from behind at walking pace is stepped out of

- **The balance assist lets go of a body creeping off its feet that no step
  is called for** (#750, `ae3d.motion`). Shoved from behind at 1.0 m/s, a
  person's walking pace, the box man could take no step for 1.4 s. The
  assist slowed it under STEP_SPEED and held its centre of mass past its
  toes as it crept over them at 0.15 to 0.33 m/s: its capture point 11 to
  13 cm past them, under STEP_MARGIN, its centre of mass 29 cm ahead of its
  ankles, leaning 0.31 rad. Its step came too late and it fell. The assist
  faded only for a trunk nearly still; it now also fades for a body
  standing whose centre of mass is past its feet and going further while
  its capture point is within STEP_MARGIN, the gap neither rule covered.
  Let go, it tips, and its capture point calls the step in time. It holds
  as before past the margin, where the step is called and leans on it;
  while staggering, a step under way or a hand braced; for a second after
  a rise, the way up's speed still in the body; and for a body leaning on
  anything but its feet (a wall, a car, another figure), which its feet are
  not all that hold.
  - tests/test_balance: shoved from behind at 1.0 m/s at 13 angles (-30
    to 30 by 5), fresh and up again from a fall onto its back, it goes
    down in 1 of 26 (main: 5).
  - A sweep of 576 shoves (72 directions, 0.8 to 1.4 m/s, fresh and up
    again from a fall): 10 go down, main 18; 6% more steps.
  - tests/test_struck_walker: at one of its 21 moments the walker, its
    catch steps over, stood leaning past its feet and tipped over with
    nothing touching it (#718); now it steps out of the lean. Its check is
    that it never goes down twice (#684).

### A foot that carries the body is not lifted to step aside (#705)

- **Stepping aside, the body leans onto the other foot first**
  (`ae3d.motion`). A third of a side stagger's swinging feet dragged on
  the ground, 1.36 times the body's weight on them as they went, and the
  drag twisted the body off its heading. A foot about to step aside that
  still carries 30% of the weight now waits while the body leans onto the
  other: the pelvis carried over that ankle at 0.6 m/s, both legs solved
  down to where the feet stand, until the foot carries under 20%, 0.15 s
  at the most (LEAN_LOAD, LEAN_SPEED, LEAN_UNLOADED, LEAN_LONGEST). A
  person shifts their weight before a voluntary step the same way.
  - Of 75 side shoves of 1.2 to 1.6 m/s, the box man fell in 2 in 319
    steps (before it 7 in 398; main 6 in 415), and the IK rig stood turned
    4.3 degrees off its heading on average, 27 at most (6.8 and 62; main
    5.9 and 63). Carried at 1.5 m/s the box man turned up to 29 degrees;
    with the swing's landing aimed 50 or 100 ms later for the lean, the IK
    rig fell in 3 and 8.

### Heading after a hard side shove (#752)

Shoved hard from the side, a figure can stand up again turned off its
heading: as the other leg crosses in front, the stance foot spins on the
ground, the swinging foot still carrying weight drags, and the friction
twists the body (#752, traced). Over 30 sets of test_stagger's side
shoves, each moved up to 0.05 m/s either way, the worst turn a set reads
11 degrees by the median against main's 12, 16 at the 80th percentile
against 15; one side shove in 100 falls on both.

- **tests/test_stagger holds every shove but the hardest side one to 10
  degrees** off its heading (#689). The 1.6 m/s side shove's turn is
  chance: past 10 degrees in 13 of those 30 sets on main, up to 55; it is
  printed and left to #752.
